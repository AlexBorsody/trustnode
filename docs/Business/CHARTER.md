# Trust Commons Charter

TrustNode and SourceSelect. Commons principles supporting the
[canonical product strategy](STRATEGY.md).

## I. Show your work

Every claim must be traceable through its sources, evidence, and confidence derivation.

## II. Keep the commons open

Methodology, data, and code are public, versioned, reproducible, and forkable.

## III. Trust is never for sale

Rankings and trust assessments cannot be bought, sponsored, or boosted.

## IV. Make influence accountable

If the system's influence changes what it measures, revise and version the
methodology publicly. Do not hide changes to scoring or ranking.

## V. Label community influence

Community evidence, votes, and source packs are visible, contestable inputs.
They never silently rewrite canonical confidence. Votes are signals, not verdicts.

## VI. AI explains; it does not decide

Algorithms calculate from evidence. AI may summarize or explain, but never sets
the trust numbers.

## VII. The business serves the commons

Revenue comes from services, implementation, and deployments around the commons.
Commercial interests must not corrupt its trust assessments.

## VIII. Preserve continuity

Data, methodology, and code must remain available for others to continue the
project independently of its founder.

## IX. Compute trust in the open

The long-term trust model uses source relationships and evidence. Seeds, weights,
and algorithms are public, versioned, and disputable. Seeds are starting points,
not permanent authorities. Community source packs make curation visible; graph
authority must be explained and distinguished from measured factual accuracy.
